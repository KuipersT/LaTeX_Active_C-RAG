\section{Active C-RAG}\label{sec:approach}

In this section we introduce Active C-RAG (AC-RAG), which follows the general block diagram in Figure~\ref{fig:system}, incorporating iterative evidence retrieval and subclaim editing under the requirement \eqref{eq:fdr}.

\subsection{Subclaim Scorer}\label{subsec:scorer}

We start by adapting the subclaim score \eqref{eq:crag_score} to settings like Figure~\ref{fig:system}, that retrieve evidence not only based on the query $x$ but also based on intermediate subclaims. To this end, we leverage a natural language inference (NLI) model. For a passage $d$ and a subclaim $c$, the NLI model \tk{REFS}, which returns the probabilities $p_{\mathrm{ent}}(d, c)$, $p_{\mathrm{con}}(d, c)$ and $p_{\mathrm{neu}}(d, c)$ that document $d$ entails, contradicts, or is neutral about subclaim $c$, with $p_{\mathrm{ent}}(\cdot) + p_{\mathrm{con}}(\cdot) + p_{\mathrm{neu}}(\cdot) = 1$. The scorer then assigns two scores $s_t = [s^{\mathrm{ent}}_{t}, s^{\mathrm{con}}_{t}]$ to each subclaim $c \in \hat{y}_t$, namely
\begin{equation}
  s^{\mathrm{ent}}_t(c \mid x, D_t) = \max_{d \in D_t} p_{\mathrm{ent}}(d, c) \quad \text{and} \quad s^{\mathrm{con}}_t(c \mid x, D_t) = \max_{d \in D_t} p_{\mathrm{con}}(d, c).
  \label{eq:nli_score}
\end{equation}
These scores depend only on whether some document $d \in D_t$ entails or contradicts $c$, and they do not depend on how the query is worded, making it easier to extract evidence from later rounds. Entailment-based scorers have also been found to match or outperform LLM confidence scores for conformal factuality in RAG, at a far lower computational cost \cite{chen2026conformal}.

The scorer also returns the evidence that each round adds for each subclaim. For a subclaim $c$, let $N_t(c) \subseteq D_t$ denote the passages of $D_t$ that have not been scored against $c$ in earlier rounds. The new evidence for $c$ at round $t$ is then the best of these passages,
\begin{equation}
  p_t(c) = \max_{d \in N_t(c)} p_{\mathrm{ent}}(d, c),
  \label{eq:new_score}
\end{equation}
which is only defined when $N_t(c) \neq \emptyset$. A passage therefore contributes to $p_t(c)$ at most once for each subclaim, including a passage that was retrieved for a different subclaim.

\subsection{Per-Round E-Values}\label{subsec:evalues}

To decide which subclaims can be returned, AC-RAG measures the evidence for each subclaim by means of e-values~\cite{vovk2021evalues,ramdas2023game}. For each subclaim $c$, we consider the null hypothesis $H_c$ that $c$ is unsupported, i.e., $a(c \mid x) = 0$. An e-value for $H_c$ is a non-negative statistic whose expected value is at most $1$ when $H_c$ holds. A large e-value therefore provides evidence against $H_c$, since, by Markov's inequality, the e-value of an unsupported subclaim exceeds $1/\alpha$ with probability at most $\alpha$. Unlike p-values, e-values obtained from successive rounds of evidence can be combined into a single measure of evidence, and e-values of many subclaims can be combined regardless of the dependence among them.

The e-values of AC-RAG are obtained by comparing the evidence for a subclaim with the evidence found for the unsupported subclaims of labelled calibration queries. As the evidence available for a subclaim changes over the rounds, this comparison is made separately at each round, against calibration queries that have reached the same stage of the loop. To this end, the calibration set is partitioned into $T + 1$ disjoint subsets $\mathcal{C}_0, \ldots, \mathcal{C}_T$, where subset $\mathcal{C}_t$ contains $n_t$ labelled queries that are processed by the same loop as the test query up to round $t$. Since the decisions taken before round $t$ do not depend on $\mathcal{C}_t$, the test query and the calibration queries in $\mathcal{C}_t$ receive the same treatment up to round $t$, and they are therefore exchangeable at round $t$.

Following the e-scores of~\cite{dhillon2026escores}, at each round $t$ at which the new evidence $p_t(c)$ in \eqref{eq:new_score} is defined, we evaluate the per-round e-value
\begin{equation}
  e_t(c) = \frac{(n_t + 1)\, p_t(c)}{p_t(c) + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_t} p^*_{i,t}},
  \label{eq:round_evalue}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  p^*_{i,t} = \max \bigl\{ p_t(c') : c' \in \hat{y}_{t,i},\ a(c' \mid x_i) = 0 \bigr\}
  \label{eq:cal_max}
\end{equation}
is the new evidence of the most convincing unsupported subclaim among the subclaims $\hat{y}_{t,i}$ of calibration query $x_i \in \mathcal{C}_t$ at round $t$, with $p^*_{i,t} = 0$ if there is none. The e-value \eqref{eq:round_evalue} is large when the new evidence for $c$ is strong compared with the evidence that the most convincing unsupported subclaims of the calibration queries receive at the same round, and it is small otherwise. Since the test query is compared with the most convincing unsupported subclaim of each calibration query, the largest e-value among the unsupported subclaims of the test query satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}\Bigl[ \max_{c \in \hat{y}_t :\, a(c \mid x) = 0} e_t(c) \Bigr] \leq 1
\end{equation}
by exchangeability~\cite{dhillon2026escores}. Each $e_t(c)$ is therefore a valid e-value for $H_c$, regardless of the number of subclaims and of the dependence among them. Note that $e_t(c) \leq n_t + 1$, so that a single round can only provide a limited amount of evidence.

\subsection{E-Processes}\label{subsec:eprocess}

A supported subclaim may only receive strong evidence after several rounds, e.g., once the passage that entails it has been retrieved. AC-RAG therefore accumulates the per-round e-values of each subclaim into an e-process~\cite{ramdas2023game}, i.e., a sequence of e-values that remains valid at whichever round the system stops. The e-process $M_t(c)$ starts at $1$ at the round at which $c$ is created, and at every round at which $e_t(c)$ is evaluated, it is updated as
\begin{equation}
  M_t(c) = M_{t-1}(c) \bigl( 1 + \lambda_t \bigl( e_t(c) - 1 \bigr) \bigr),
  \label{eq:eprocess_update}
\end{equation}
while it is left unchanged at the other rounds. Following~\cite{waudbysmith2023betting,zecchin2024adaptive}, the e-process can be interpreted as the wealth of a gambler who bets against $H_c$. At each round, the gambler bets a fraction $\lambda_t \in [0, 1]$ of the current wealth on $c$ being supported, and the payoff $e_t(c) - 1$ is positive when the new evidence for $c$ is strong, and negative otherwise. The wealth therefore grows over the rounds in which strong evidence is found for $c$, while the fraction $1 - \lambda_t$ that is not bet is kept, so that a single round with weak evidence does not discard the evidence of the previous rounds. Since $e_t(c) \geq 0$, the wealth never becomes negative, and the stake $\lambda_t$ may be chosen based on the previous rounds, e.g., as in~\cite{waudbysmith2023betting}.
% Note that the payoff $e_t(c) - 1$ has a non-positive mean when $c$ is unsupported, since $e_t(c)$ is a valid e-value. 
Constructing such a payoff requires the calibration queries, since the null hypothesis $H_c$, unlike a hypothesis on the mean of an observed loss, does not by itself constrain the distribution of the NLI scores.

If each per-round e-value is valid given the previous rounds, i.e., $\mathbb{E}[e_t(c) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \leq 1$ for every unsupported subclaim $c$, where $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$ denotes everything observed up to round $t - 1$, then each update \eqref{eq:eprocess_update} does not increase $M_t(c)$ in expectation for an unsupported subclaim. The value $M_{t^*}(c)$ at any stopping time $t^*$ is then a valid e-value for $H_c$, i.e., $\mathbb{E}[M_{t^*}(c)] \leq 1$~\cite{ramdas2023game}, which is what allows the system to decide, based on what it has observed, whether to stop or to continue for further rounds.

The scores of a subclaim are dependent across rounds, since the query $x$ and the subclaim $c$ are reused at every round, the subclaims are rewritten based on past evidence, and the subclaims that are reworked at round $t$ are selected by the decisions of the previous rounds. \subsection{Decision Block}\label{subsec:decision}

The decision block first removes the subclaims that the evidence contradicts. To this end, a subclaim is removed when at least one passage contradicts it and no passage entails it, which, in terms of the scores \eqref{eq:nli_score}, gives
\begin{equation}
  z_t = \bigl\{ c \in \hat{y}_t \setminus k_{t-1} : s^{\mathrm{con}}_t(c \mid x, D_t) > 1/2 \ \text{and} \ s^{\mathrm{ent}}_t(c \mid x, D_t) \leq 1/2 \bigr\},
  \label{eq:removal}
\end{equation}
with $k_{t-1} = \emptyset$ for $t = 0$. Since the three probabilities of the NLI model sum to $1$, a probability above $1/2$ is the largest of the three, so that a passage with $p_{\mathrm{con}}(d, c) > 1/2$ contradicts $c$, and $s^{\mathrm{ent}}_t(c \mid x, D_t) \leq 1/2$ means that no passage entails $c$ with a probability above $1/2$. We note that removal requires no calibration, as a removed subclaim is never returned, and thus cannot lead to a false discovery in \eqref{eq:fdr}. Accordingly, the e-process of a removed subclaim is set to $0$, which preserves its validity.

The remaining subclaims are kept or reworked based on their e-processes. Selecting several subclaims requires care, since every additional subclaim that is accepted is another opportunity to accept an unsupported one. To this end, AC-RAG uses the e-BH procedure~\cite{wang2022false}, which is the e-value counterpart of the Benjamini--Hochberg procedure~\cite{benjamini1995controlling}. Given e-values for $K$ hypotheses, sorted in decreasing order as $E_{(1)} \geq E_{(2)} \geq \ldots \geq E_{(K)}$, the e-BH procedure, at level $\alpha$, selects the $\hat{k}$ hypotheses with the largest e-values, where
\begin{equation}
\hat{k} = \max \Bigl\{ k : E_{(k)} \geq \frac{K}{\alpha k} \Bigr\},
\label{eq:ebh}
\end{equation}
with $\hat{k} = 0$ if no such $k$ exists, and we denote $\mathrm{EBH}_{\alpha}(\cdot)$ as the selected set. The threshold $K/(\alpha k)$ decreases with the number $k$ of selected hypotheses, since a single unsupported subclaim represents a smaller fraction of a larger selected set. The e-BH procedure controls the FDR at level $\alpha$ as long as each e-value is valid on its own, regardless of the dependence among them~\cite{wang2022false}, which is important here, as the subclaims of an answer share the query $x$ and the evidence $D_t$. Moreover, since the value of each e-process at a stopping time is a valid e-value, the e-BH procedure can be applied to the e-processes at whichever round the system stops. In AC-RAG, $K$ is an upper bound on the number of subclaims that the system may create, which is fixed in advance, and the e-values of the subclaims that have been removed, rewritten or not created are set to $0$.

The subclaims selected by the e-BH procedure at round $t$ are kept, i.e.,
\begin{equation}
  k_t = k_{t-1} \cup \bigl( \mathrm{EBH}_{\alpha}\bigl(\{ M_t(c) \}\bigr) \setminus z_t \bigr),
  \label{eq:keep}
\end{equation}
and every subclaim that is neither kept nor removed is reworked, i.e., $w_t = \hat{y}_t \setminus (k_t \cup z_t)$. A kept subclaim is no longer reworked, which agrees with the subclaims of $k_t$ being kept unchanged by the revision block, and protects a subclaim that is already well supported from being reworded. All subclaims of $w_t$ accumulate evidence in parallel. In the example, $c^{\star}_1$ is entailed by the Jaws article, so that $e_0(c^{\star}_1)$ is large and $c^{\star}_1$ may be selected by \eqref{eq:ebh}, whereas no passage of $D_0$ entails $c^{\star}_2$, so that $e_0(c^{\star}_2)$ is small and $c^{\star}_2$ is reworked.

The decision block stops at the round
\begin{equation}
  t^* = \min\bigl\{ t : w_t = \emptyset, \ \text{or} \ D_{t+1} = D_t, \ \text{or} \ t = T \bigr\},
  \label{eq:stop}
\end{equation}
that is, when every subclaim has been kept or removed, when the rework returns no new passages, so that no further evidence can be gained, or when the budget of $T$ rounds is used up.As the e-processes remain valid at any stopping time, any stopping rule preserves \eqref{eq:fdr}, and \eqref{eq:stop} only determines how much evidence is collected.

\subsection{Query Generation}\label{subsec:query}

For each subclaim $c \in w_t$, an LLM writes the retrieval query $q_t(c)$ from the query $x$, the subclaim $c$ and the evidence $D_t$, so that the query can make use of a bridge entity identified in earlier rounds, and the returned passages are added to the evidence store as in \eqref{eq:store}, so that the query $q_t(x, w_t, D_t)$ of Section~\ref{sec:prob_form} consists of one query for each subclaim. In the example, $c^{\star}_2$ yields a query such as ``Steven Spielberg birthplace'', which retrieves his biography. As a simpler alternative, the subclaim $c$ itself can be used as the retrieval query. Unlike in multi-hop C-RAG, whose retrieval hops are driven by the query $x$, the queries of AC-RAG are written from the subclaims that are not yet kept.

\subsection{Subclaim Revision}\label{subsec:revision}

The revision block keeps the subclaims of $k_t$ unchanged, and, for each subclaim $c \in w_t$, either leaves $c$ unchanged or rewrites it given the new evidence. It may also add new subclaims that the new evidence supports. A rewritten or added subclaim is a new hypothesis, with an e-process that starts at $1$, and the e-process of the subclaim before rewriting is set to $0$, as for a removed subclaim. A refinement of a kept subclaim, such as the month of an event whose year is already kept, is also added as a new subclaim, so that the kept subclaim keeps its status. In the example, $c^{\star}_2$ needs no rewriting, as Spielberg's biography entails it as it stands, so that $c^{\star}_2$ keeps its e-process, which grows with the new evidence until $c^{\star}_2$ is kept. \tk{as we discussed, it might be worth ignoring this for now}

\subsection{Final Filtering Step}\label{subsec:final}

At the stopping time $t^*$, the final filtering block returns the subclaims selected by the e-BH procedure \eqref{eq:ebh} applied to the e-processes, i.e.,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} = \mathrm{EBH}_{\alpha}\bigl(\{ M_{t^*}(c) \}\bigr).
  \label{eq:final_ebh}
\end{equation}
Since $M_{t^*}(c)$ is a valid e-value for every unsupported subclaim $c$, and the e-BH procedure controls the FDR for arbitrarily dependent e-values, the returned set \eqref{eq:final_ebh} satisfies \eqref{eq:fdr} for any stopping time $t^*$. With $T = 0$, AC-RAG reduces to applying the e-BH procedure to the e-values \eqref{eq:round_evalue} of round $0$.
